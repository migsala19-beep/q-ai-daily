English
Two trends stand out. First, decentralized/distributed AI compute is becoming a key path to lowering costs. SkyPilot unifies fragmented compute across clouds and clusters into "one AI supercomputer," freeing teams from vendor lock-in, while Petals uses a BitTorrent-style P2P network so home users can jointly run large LLMs with up to 10× faster inference/fine-tuning than offloading. This signals AI infrastructure shifting from centralized clouds toward collaborative edge computing. Second, multi-agent systems are moving from general chat into professional engineering workflows. AINTMA proposes an agentic architecture for test management, combining generative intelligence, secure cloud communication, and adaptive quality analytics to enable autonomous decision-making in distributed cloud environments. This marks a shift from scripted QA automation to autonomous QA agents. Finally, LLM reliability in vertical domains deserves attention: medical watermarking evaluation shows general-purpose watermarks may fail in critical fields, while ClickGuard demonstrates a browser extension that uses informativeness metrics and LLMs to detect and spoil clickbait.
