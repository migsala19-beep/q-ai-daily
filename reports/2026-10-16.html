English
Three of today's five arXiv papers converge on one theme: autonomous agents are moving from "can they do tasks" to "how they improve and how we honestly measure them." ClawLoop introduces a harness-native training loop where LLM agents self-improve inside sandbox workspaces (file systems + shells), reducing reliance on human-labeled post-training data. Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench 2.0 automates diverse long-horizon task construction to measure sustained agent progress rather than single-task success. In contrast, "The Silent Failures in Agentic Data Science" offers a cold reality check: current data science agents suffer widespread unnoticed failures from task formulation through execution, revealing a gap between automation promises and reliable delivery. A second direction is the philosophical dilemma of evaluation: "Artificiality's First Exam" argues that any benchmark calibrated against humans or current AI saturates quickly or becomes circular, proposing an LLM-referenced C-test for potentially superintelligent systems—directly relevant whenever AI evaluates AI, as in our own daily briefing pipeline. On GitHub, security tools like gitleaks and Ghidra trending reflects growing enterprise anxiety over secret leakage and code auditing in the AI code-generation era.
